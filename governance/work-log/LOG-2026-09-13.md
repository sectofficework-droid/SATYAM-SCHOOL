# LOG-2026-09-13 — Kiosk face scan freezing: found the likely cause, added debug tooling

## What I set out to do

You reported that the attendance kiosk's face scan isn't working — the
camera freezes, then restarts over and over — and asked me to debug it.

## What I found

I think the camera isn't actually broken. I think the app is too busy to
draw it.

Here's the shape of it. While the scan screen is open, the app takes a
photo every second and then checks it five different ways: is there a face,
are the eyes open, is the lighting usable, is it blurry, is it a real person
rather than a photo, and who is it. The problem is that each of those checks
opens and unpacks the photo file from scratch, on its own. So every single
second, the same photo gets unpacked five separate times.

All of that work happens on the same thread that draws the screen. While
it's running, the app can't paint anything — so the camera preview stops
updating and looks frozen. It isn't frozen, it's just not being redrawn.

And because a new photo is requested every second regardless, the camera
keeps being told to take another shot while it's still behind on the last
one. That's a good candidate for the "restarting again and again" part, and
on a virtual device like BlueStacks the camera can genuinely give up under
that load.

To be clear about confidence: this is from reading the code carefully, not
from watching it fail. **I could not run it** — BlueStacks wasn't running
and no device was plugged in, so there was nothing to install onto. That's
exactly why I built the measuring tools below instead of guessing further.

This is also **not** the same freeze we fixed on 9 September. That one only
happened if you backed out of the "enter code" screen a particular way. This
one needs no navigation at all — it happens just standing at the scan screen.

## What I did

I added debugging instrumentation to the kiosk face-scan screen. It does not
change how scanning behaves. It only records what's happening, and it's
switched off entirely in the real (release) app, so nothing here can slow
down or alter the build you give to staff.

Three things it now records:

- **Where the time goes.** Every stage of a scan is timed and logged, so we
  can see which step eats the second, and how many times the photo got
  unpacked in one attempt.
- **Whether the app is actually stuck.** A small heartbeat runs in the
  background. If it's late, that lateness *is* the freeze, measured directly.
  This is the piece that proves or disproves my theory outright.
- **What the camera is doing.** Every time the camera starts, restarts,
  fails, or gets torn down when the app goes to the background, it's logged
  with an ID so we can tell one camera session from the next.

I also added a small readout at the bottom of the scan screen, visible only
in the debug build, showing the last dozen lines. That's so you can walk up
to the kiosk, trigger the freeze, and just read what happened — without
having a laptop plugged in.

I checked it compiles cleanly and built the debug app successfully.

## What I did NOT do

I have not fixed anything. The rule book says I don't rewrite working
production code without you explicitly approving that specific change, and
the fix touches the heart of the face-recognition pipeline. So the diagnosis
and the proposed fix are written down and waiting for your go-ahead.

My recommendation, smallest first:

1. Unpack the photo once per scan instead of five times. Same checks, same
   results, a fifth of the work. Low risk.
2. Don't ask for a new photo while the previous one is still being processed.
   Low risk.
3. Move the heavy image work off the drawing thread. This is the one that
   properly stops the freezing, but it's a real change to production code, so
   I'd rather do 1 and 2, re-measure, and then decide.

## Where things stand

The debug build is compiled and ready to install. Nothing is committed to
git — the changes are sitting on disk for you to review.

## Update — same day, you confirmed it's real, so I kept going

You told me the delay happens the same way on a real Android device, not
just the test emulator, and to just fix it rather than wait. So I did,
right on the emulator.

**Two more targeted fixes, both tested and ruled out:**

- Locking the camera's focus and exposure instead of letting it
  re-adjust every shot. First attempt crashed the camera screen entirely on
  one camera — some cameras don't support this at all, so I made it fail
  safely instead (falls back to normal behavior, doesn't crash). Where it
  did work, it made no difference to the delay.
- Switching to the older camera engine Android offers. This one was
  actually worse — slower than before, and it also displayed the camera
  preview sideways. Reverted it.

At that point I'd ruled out flash, focus locking, and the camera engine
itself. All three point at the same thing: asking the camera for "one
high-quality photo" is just slow here, no matter how it's configured.

**So I made the bigger change.** Instead of asking the camera for a fresh
photo every second, the app now reads directly from the camera's live
video feed, which is built to be fast by nature — no "take a photo" step at
all. I checked with you before doing this since it's a real rewrite of core
scanning logic, not a small settings tweak, and you said to go ahead.

This required teaching the app to read the camera's raw video format
directly and rotate it correctly by hand, work the original code
deliberately avoided when it was first built, precisely because getting
that rotation math wrong could quietly break face recognition instead of
just being slow. I did this carefully and it compiles cleanly.

**Where I had to stop:** the test emulator disconnected right as I was
about to verify it, and I have no way to restart it myself. So this
rewrite is written and compiles, but **I have not seen it run.** I want to
be direct about that: everything about whether it actually works is
unverified. I am not calling this fixed.

**Next step:** reconnect the emulator (or the real tablet) so I can install
this build and watch it actually scan a face. That's the only way to know
whether the rotation math is right and whether it's actually fast now.

## Update — same day, verified: it works

You said BlueStacks was back up. I installed it and watched it run for
real this time.

**It's fixed.** Before, the camera sat idle for four to seven seconds
between each check. Now it's checking again, and again, roughly every
second like it's supposed to — each check takes well under half a second,
often closer to a tenth of one. I let it run for about 13 minutes straight
without touching it: 178 checks, not one skipped, not one camera restart.

**The riskiest part — the hand-rotated video frames — also checked out.**
I watched it detect a real face twice, and both times the face crop came
out right-side-up, not sideways. More convincingly: both attempts scored
close to the actual match threshold against the real enrolled staff
records — one just under, one just barely over but not distinct enough
from a second-place score to count. That kind of near-miss only happens if
the geometry is basically correct. A wrong rotation would have produced
garbage, not a close call.

**One honest new finding, unrelated to anything I touched:** once a face
is actually found, looking it up against the enrolled staff list itself
takes about six seconds over the network. That was always true — it used
to be hidden behind the camera's own five-to-seven-second delay, so nobody
could tell the two apart. It's not what you reported, and it only affects
the brief "verifying" moment after a face is found, not the idle waiting
that was the actual complaint. I'm flagging it, not fixing it — that would
be new, separate scope.

**Where things stand:** the freeze you reported is fixed and verified.
Nothing has been committed to git yet — it's all sitting staged, ready
when you want it in. My one remaining recommendation, not a blocker: try
it once on the actual physical kiosk tablet before this goes live, since
everything so far was proven on the test emulator.

## Update — same day, one more thing tried and ruled out

You asked how to fix the camera delay, and then told me it was still
behaving the same as before — which lined up exactly with what I'd
flagged: the fix I'd applied never touched the part that's actually slow.

I found a specific, well-known cause: the camera library this app uses
defaults to leaving the flash on "auto," which on this phone's camera
system can add a multi-second delay before every photo while it checks
whether it needs to flash. I turned that off and tested it directly.

**It didn't work.** The timing came back exactly the same as before, four
to six seconds every time. So that wasn't the cause after all. I kept the
change anyway since it's harmless and correct regardless — this camera has
no flash hardware to begin with — but it isn't the fix.

I checked one other lever inside the camera library and found the app has
no way to control it without modifying the library itself, which isn't
something to do lightly.

At that point I'd tried two things on the test emulator without success,
and that emulator's camera is already known to be unreliable from past
sessions on this project. I asked how you wanted to proceed, and you chose
the right call: stop guessing on the emulator and test the real kiosk
tablet directly.

**Where things stand:** nothing is confirmed broken on real hardware yet.
It's entirely possible the four-to-six-second delay is specific to the test
emulator's fake camera and the real tablet is fine. We won't know until we
try it there.

**Next step:** connect the real kiosk tablet (with USB debugging turned on)
